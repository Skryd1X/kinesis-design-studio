# Art direction and visual craft

Read the product before selecting its visual language. Identify the user's task, environment, audience, content, actual brand constraints and emotional register. A trading tool, fashion campaign and mobile habit app need different density, motion and composition.

Offer a small meaningful choice: each option must describe layout, typography, image treatment, palette, motion and a tradeoff. Avoid a list of empty labels. With a fixed brand, offer treatments within the brand rather than replacing it. Record the choice so it survives later implementation and repair passes.

Use a signature specific to the subject: an editorial crop, expressive type system, tactile material, functional interaction, product simulation or spatial narrative. Establish a clear focal point and reading order. Contrast includes scale, rhythm, density and negative space, not just color. Visual ambition should concentrate in the elements that earn attention; retain quiet regions that support reading and action.

Set semantic tokens for color, spacing, typography, surface, radius, depth and motion. Verify readable text and UI states in every theme. Body copy, form labels, helper text, errors and data are design content. Use clear action labels and preserve factual product copy; do not invent testimonials, customer counts or product claims.

Design the full page/flow: navigation, long content, loading, empty/error/success, focus and disabled states, mobile adaptation and the ending. A polished hero with generic or unfinished sections is incomplete. Compare against references for craft while creating an original composition; do not reproduce another brand's identity or assets without rights.

Anti-patterns are diagnostic prompts, not aesthetic prohibitions. A gradient, serif font, bento grid or dark background may be correct for a specific brief. Reject arbitrary decoration, identical cards without a reason, contrastless controls, incoherent tokens, and a design indistinguishable from the agent's default output.
